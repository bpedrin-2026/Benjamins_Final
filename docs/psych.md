
# Executive Dysfunction in Incarcerated & Formerly Incarcerated Individuals

## Section 1 — Why This Topic? 

### Step 1: Topic
<strong>Executive Dysfunction in formerly incarcerated individuals</Strong>

<em>Perceived Control</em> – due to the harsh prison environment, the simple fact of being incarcerated, the social isolation, and the exposure to potential violence,\
inmates have no perceived control over the stressors in their lives, which leads to chronic stress.

Executive Functioning is centered in the pre-frontal cortex –\
A dysfunction of the executive functioning, then, is a dysfunction of the pre-frontal cortex’s processing abilities

### Step 2: In My Own Words
- What it is:\
    There is a clear decline in the cognitive functioning of people who have spent significant time being incarcerated.\
    Upon release, the normal functioning of the brain is noticeably slowed, and it takes time and effort to readjust to the pace of the world and all its stimuli.

- Key features:\
    Decline in general self-regulation, ability to plan, working memory, taking initiative, attention and impulse control.

- What people often confuse it with (optional):\
    laziness, combativeness, insubordination and defiance, and potentially anti-social disorders.

- A quick example:\
    For example... A man serves 20 years in prison, deprived of any significant visual and/or auditory stimulation, significant social interactions, and stress relief methods.\
    Because of this deficit, this man’s cognitive abilities have been weakened, and he will now have an even greater challenge when released.\
    His ability to navigate the world has been compromised; his ability to plan... his motivation to take initiative in his own life... and his ability to self-regulate.
